## Slide 12

Quenching

Reaction (or an aliquot) is “quenched” after a certain amount of time (≥ ms).
Quenching can be carried out by mixing, eg, sudden change in pH or dilution, or by a sudden drop in temperature.
Have to be sure that quenching is fast compared to the reaction and does not cause any other changes

## Slide 26

Starting reactions with light

Before first lasers invented (1960s), light flashes could be generated in microseconds (10-6 s)
“Flash photolysis” – developed by Norrish and Porter in the 1950s – use a flash of light to break bonds, to produce reactive species (“pump” pulse)
Monitor reaction spectroscopically with another beam (“probe”) in real time.
Nowadays, lasers can easily generate nanosecond  (10-9 s) to femtosecond (10-15 s) pulses


## Slide 27

“Pump” pulse can
Break bonds to give radicals
Generate electronically excited states
Remove a (designed light-sensitive) protecting group from, e.g., an enzyme
Produce a precursor which (quickly) produces reagent
Heat; by (a) mixing a dye that absorbs at a certain wavelength, or(b) using an IR pulse tuned to, eg, OH stretch in water.

## Slide 28

Designing a pump-probe experiment

Pump beam is intense (to cause chemical change)
Probe beam is not intense (this should not cause chemical change!)
…so shine in different directions (so pump pulse doesn’t ruin your light detector)
Pump beam travels across width of cell (so ensure uniform illumination). Probe beam travels along length of cell (increase signal)

## Slide 33

Pump and probe lasers

Pump and probe are usually different lasers / light sources.
Pump has to be intense but exact wavelength less important; probe has to be “tunable” so you can vary the wavelength to probe the specific species that you want to look at
Can have pulse and probe from same laser (see later slide)
Each pump pulse usually only makes a small fraction of (pre-) reactant react, so probe only detects a very small signal. The reaction can therefore be repeated many times, adding all of the probe data, to increase the signal.
