## Slide 16

Remember: Rate Law

The Rate Law is an expression that relates reactant concentration to reaction rate
For example:   A + B  products
Rate law:    	Rate   [A]n[B]m	Rate = k[A]n[B]m
k = rate constant (independent of [ ])
n = order with respect to [A]
m = order with respect to [B]
n + m = overall order of the reaction
n and m can NOT be deduced from the overall balanced chemical reaction equation
The Rate Law must by determined by experiment, or predicted from a mechanism (we will learn how to do this!)


## Slide 17

Remember: Rate Law

Rate here means “rate of change of concentration as a function of time” – so always has units of concentration/time (e.g. M s-1, moles dm-3s-1)
Rate constants allow us to balance units to achieve this.
Rate = k[A][B]
c t-1=k  c c
M s-1=k M M
Units of k: M-1 s-1

## Slide 26

Rules for determining rate equation for multi-step reactions (2)

Many components will have elementary reactions that make them, and other elementary reactions that remove them.
Calculate the (overall) rate of change of each component:
For each elementary reaction, add the rate if it makes that component. Subtract the rate if it removes that component.
Don’t forget to multiply by the number of molecules of that component that the elementary reaction makes or removes.
