## Slide 9

Chain Reactions

So far we have considered “consecutive” multi-step reactions.
Reagents undergo a series of reactions (“elementary steps”) one leading on from another, until they make the products.
Chain reactions:  molecule of intermediate produced in one step generates a molecule of intermediate in a later step, which then generates another molecule of intermediate, and so on.
Intermediate –  “chain carrier ” – often a radical.
Gives rise to more complicated kinetic schemes, usually involving half-powers
e.g. Rate  [A]3/2

## Slide 28

Which other reactions did we ignore, and why?

## Slide 29

Initiation steps are all very endothermic, some more than others.
The more endothermic, the greater the activation barrier.
H-H bond energy 436 kJ/mol
Br-Br bond energy 193 kJ/mol

Why ignore the other initiation steps?


## Slide 30

## Slide 32

Rate constants and complicated rate equations

Chain reactions can have particularly complicated rate equations, with several rate constants
Rate normally expressed as “concentration of stuff”/”some time”, e.g. mM/s, nM/nanosecond…
Simplest case: rate (concentration/time) = k (???) [A](concentration)
Balance equation – units of k must be 1/time
To compare two rate constants, they must have the same units
What about more complicated cases?
