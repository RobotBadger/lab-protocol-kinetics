## Slide 4

Rules for determining the rate equation for a multi-step reaction (1)

We can break any reaction down to a series of one-step reactions.
Each single step – “elementary reaction” - follows simple kinetics.
For each elementary reaction, we can write the rate equation just from the chemical equation.
The order of the reaction is the same as the molecularity…


## Slide 5

Rules for determining the rateequation for a multi-step reaction (2)

Many components will have elementary reactions that make them, and other elementary reactions that remove them.
Calculate the (overall) rate of change of each component:
For each elementary reaction, add the rate if it makes that component. Subtract the rate if it removes that component.
Don’t forget to multiply by the number of molecules of that component that the elementary reaction makes or removes.

## Slide 11

What does the analogy tell us?

The level of water in the bucket initially increases, but quickly reaches a “steady state”
At steady state, (rate of water flowing in) = (rate of water flowing out)
The steady state level of water increases with rate flowing in (either because more water in A, or because the hole k1 is larger)...
…and decreases with rate constant for water flowing out (if hole k2 is larger)
